## What a gutter cleaning visit covers

Gutter cleaning is the removal of leaves, seed pods, and pine needles from the gutter runs so rain can reach the downspouts and drain away from the house. A visit from Conway Gutter Pros includes:

- Clearing the debris out of the gutter runs
- Flushing the downspouts, at no extra charge
- A free quote for the house

Nobody has to be home for it. Gate or yard access is enough.

## Terms used in these notes

- **Gutter run:** one continuous length of gutter along the roofline.
- **Downspout:** the vertical pipe that carries water from the gutter down to the ground.
- **Flushing:** running water through a downspout.
- **Stories:** the number of floors of the house.

## What sets the gutter cleaning quote

Quotes from Conway Gutter Pros are free, and the price varies with three things about the house.

- **Stories.** A one-story ranch and a two-story house are different jobs.
- **Gutter length.** The total run of gutter around the house counts.
- **How clogged the gutters are.** A run packed with a season of needles and pods is more work than one cleared in spring.

Those three change from house to house, so no single number fits every home. Details beyond them, such as payment methods or scheduling, are worth asking about when the quote is given.

## Before the visit

A few small things make a cleaning go smoothly, and none of them requires being at home.

- Make sure the gutters can be reached through the gate or the yard.
- Know how many stories the house has.
- Note any corner where water spills over the edge in a heavy rain, since that often marks a clog.

## How often gutters need cleaning in Conway

Gutter cleaning in Conway follows the trees: homes under oaks, sweetgums, and pines usually need two to three cleanings a year, in spring and late fall.

The mix of trees explains the schedule. The Encyclopedia of Arkansas says "thirty-two kinds of oaks are known to grow in the state," and it notes that loblolly pine has been planted throughout Arkansas. The Arkansas Department of Agriculture's tree recommendations describe sweetgum with the comment "ball-shaped pods litter the ground." Pines add needles. In a September 2023 release from the University of Arkansas extension service, forester Vic Ford said pines shed needles every fall and that drought had accelerated the drop that year.

A late-fall visit follows that drop, and a spring visit is the other fixed point in the year. Whether a given yard lands at two cleanings or three is a question for the quote.
